## Health and respiratory safety hazards of fire debris

Attempting to clear fire debris without specialized protective gear poses acute health risks that many property owners underestimate:

- **Ultra-fine carbon particulate inhalation:** Fire soot particles measure less than 2.5 microns (PM2.5), allowing them to penetrate deep into human lung tissue and enter the bloodstream, triggering severe respiratory irritation and asthma attacks.
- **Toxic chemical residues:** Burning synthetic foams, polyurethane coatings, and modern flame-retardant plastics releases toxic compounds including dioxins, furans, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs).
- **Physical puncture and burn hazards:** Hidden among soot-blackened debris are razor-sharp melted plastic shards, shattered glass fragments, and protruding drywall screws that easily pierce ordinary tennis shoes or thin garden gloves.
- **Structural instability:** High temperatures can weaken false ceiling suspension wires, warp metal stud framing, and cause drywall panels to collapse unexpectedly during handling.

Our clearance crews are outfitted with certified personal protective equipment, including N95/P100 particulate respirators, heavy puncture-resistant work gloves, protective Tyvek overalls, and steel-toe safety boots.
